**Where:** `services/api/app/idempotency.py:111` (the lock is taken with the
actor loaded before it); `services/api/app/services/tasks.py:70`
(`_current_actor`), `services/family.py` (`_locked_leader`),
`services/auth.py` (`_locked_user`); `services/pets.py` — create, patch,
weights and health events have none
**Spec:** `03` §1.2 ("before any check or write"); `04` §5 RB-3, added with
this review
**Problem:** the role and the enabled state that decide a mutation must be
the ones read after the family lock is taken. Each service does that by
hand. Two consequences:

- On the three task routes nothing tests it: without `_current_actor`, or
  without its `disabled_at` check, every test passes.
- The pet routes a member may call do not do it at all, because no role is
  involved there: a member removed while their request waits for the lock
  still has it written, as `created_by` a disabled user.

**Fix:** do it once, in the idempotency wrapper, right after it takes the
lock: read the actor again, answer `401` if it is disabled, and hand the
fresh row to the handler. The per-service copies used by ⟳ routes then go;
the writers that do not use the wrapper (`POST /invites`, the auth routes)
keep theirs. Test it once, over the matrix, as RB-3 says, so the routes of
P2-10 onward are covered as they are added.
