## R1 — minor — a number that is not finite is a 500            FIXED

**Where:** `services/api/app/schemas/body.py` (`RequestBody`); seen on
`POST /walks/{id}/finish`
**Spec:** `04` §1 (Bodies), as corrected with this review
**Problem:** a body sent with a literal `NaN` in a route or preview point
passes the parser and the schema — Python's JSON reader accepts it and a
float field allows it — and the insert into `jsonb` fails: `500`. The app
cannot produce it (JavaScript serialises `NaN` as `null`), but a crafted
request can, as with U+0000 in P2-8. `Infinity` in `distance_m` is already
`422`, by the decimal bounds. The spec never mentioned non-finite numbers.
**Fix:** refuse them in the one place that already walks every body for
U+0000, at any depth → `422`. Test: `NaN` and `Infinity` in a route point, in
a preview point, and in one numeric field of a route of each other router.

FIXED in 31ba1ac.

The shared `RequestBody` traversal now rejects non-finite floats alongside
U+0000, before field parsing, including nested lists and dictionaries.
Integers are not converted to floats, so arbitrarily large finite integers
remain safe for the shared traversal; column bounds still validate them.

Added 58 R1 cases to `tests/api/test_walks.py`: NaN, Infinity and -Infinity
in every route and preview coordinate; one field of every JSON-body router
(also me, weights and health events); a nested recurrence interval; scalar,
list and deeply nested values; and a finite-number control. The route
cases assert the shared body error, and rejected walk finishes leave the
walks, routes, mutation records and family revision unchanged. Q-17 records
how the cases cover routers with no numeric fields or no request bodies.

Removed only the non-finite branch and ran the 58 cases: 57 failed on
assertions, the finite-number control passed, and there were no setup
errors or skips. Restored the branch before running the gates.

## Assumptions and questions

- `ended_at` earlier than `started_at`, a negative `paused_ms` and a
  latitude out of range are accepted. Accepted on purpose: `04` §12 lists the
  limits, and `03` §7 says a walk recorded offline must never be rejected
  later — a phone whose clock moved during a walk would otherwise lose it.
- Finishing with an empty route stores an empty `walk_route` row. Accepted:
  `has_route` is false and the route answers `404`.
- Discarding a finished walk keeps its `walk_route` row, and the route is
  still served for the tombstone. Accepted; nothing shows a discarded walk.
